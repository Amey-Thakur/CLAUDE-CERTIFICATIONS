# How the Claude certifications compare

Where the Claude program sits against the other AI and cloud certifications
people weigh it against, and the one difference that matters more than cost.

> [!NOTE]
> Every figure about the Claude program on this page is checked against
> Anthropic's own exam guides. Figures about other vendors' programs are not
> Anthropic's to publish and change without notice, so each is dated and
> attributed, and you should confirm at the source before deciding anything.
> Where this page and a vendor's own page disagree, the vendor is right.

---

## The difference that decides it

Almost every comparison of AI certifications argues about price and syllabus.
For the Claude program, neither is the deciding factor.

**You cannot sit a Claude certification exam as an individual.**

Registration requires signing in with a company email on a domain recognized in
your organization's Claude Partner Network record. Personal addresses do not
work. AWS, Google Cloud and Microsoft certifications are open to anyone with a
payment method.

That single difference reorders the whole comparison. For most people reading
this, the practical question is not whether the Claude exam is better value
than an AWS one. It is whether they are eligible to sit it at all. See
[registration](registration.md) for the mechanics, and
[is it worth it](is-it-worth-it.md) for what to do if you are not.

## Structural comparison

The rows below are chosen because they change slowly. Fees and syllabus details
move; these do not.

| | Claude certifications | Typical cloud AI certifications |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Who may sit** | Claude Partner Network organizations only, verified by company email domain | Open to anyone |
| **Credential validity** | 12 months | Commonly 2 to 3 years. AWS states 3 years for its AI Practitioner ([source](https://aws.amazon.com/certification/recertification/)) |
| **Renewal** | Free, open book, not proctored | Usually a paid exam, sometimes a free assessment |
| **Delivery** | Pearson VUE, online or test center | Usually Pearson VUE or PSI |
| **Format** | Multiple choice and multiple response, 120 minutes | Usually multiple choice, 90 to 180 minutes |
| **Practical component** | None | None at foundational level; some professional tracks include hands-on labs |
| **Scope** | One vendor's models, products, and protocol | An entire cloud platform, of which AI is one part |
| **Preparation material** | All courses free, blueprint published with domain weights | Usually free courses plus paid practice exams |
| **Official practice exam** | None. Retired in the June 2026 migration | Usually available, often paid |
| **Fee range** | 99 to 175 USD, before partner discounts | Broadly 100 to 300 USD depending on level |

## What the Claude program does better

**The blueprint is unusually specific.** Every domain carries a published
percentage weight. Three of the four papers publish weights down to a tenth of a
percent. Most certification blueprints list domains without saying how much each
is worth, which leaves candidates to guess where to spend their time. See
[the weights](learning-paths.md).

**Every preparation course is free.** All twenty-four Claude Academy courses
cost nothing and need no partner account. You can learn the entire syllabus
before deciding whether to certify, which is not true of programs that gate
their training behind a subscription.

**The short validity is honest.** Twelve months looks unattractive beside a
three-year credential until you consider the subject. A three-year-old
certification in a field this young would be claiming more than it could
support. The free renewal keeps the cost of that honesty low.

**Scenario-based papers.** Architect Foundations presents four production
scenarios drawn at random from six published ones, and every question sits
inside a scenario. That is closer to the work than a series of unrelated
questions.

## What it does worse

**Recognition.** An AWS or Azure certification is understood by every recruiter.
CCDV-F is not, yet. Inside the partner ecosystem it carries weight; outside it,
you will spend a sentence explaining what it is.

**Eligibility.** Covered above, and it is the real barrier.

**No official practice exam.** The previous platform's was retired in the
June 2026 migration and has not been replaced. The sample questions inside each
exam guide are the only official items that exist. This repository's
[practice engine](quiz.md) exists because of that gap, but original questions
written from a blueprint are not the same thing as official ones.

**Narrow scope.** The credential says you know Claude. It does not say you know
how to run infrastructure, secure a network, or manage a data platform.

## Can they be combined?

Yes, and the combination is stronger than either alone.

A cloud certification says you can run systems. A Claude certification says you
know this model family, its API, its agent tooling, and its failure modes in
particular. Two of the Claude papers assume cloud context directly: the
deployment-platform courses cover running Claude on Amazon Bedrock and on Google
Cloud's Vertex AI, which is exactly where a cloud credential and this one meet.

If you already hold a cloud certification and work with Claude, the Developer or
Architect papers add the specific layer your existing credential does not cover.

## If you are not eligible

This is the most common outcome, and it does not have to be a dead end.

Every course is free and open to anyone. The blueprints are published. The
sample questions in the exam guides are public. You can work through the entire
syllabus, learn the material properly, and hold everything the certification
would have taught you, without ever sitting the exam.

The credential is the only part gated behind partner membership. The knowledge
is not.
